Copy Strategy - Vincent Chan

Built: 2026-05-16 Frame: Voice, brand pillars, headline bank, and the framing AG1 writes to across the cohort landing, course-page rebuild, email sequences, VSL scripts, and ad creative.

Voice DNA

Vincent's public voice across YouTube long-form, IG carousels, the Sunday newsletter, and the Entrepreneur quit-piece:

  • Plain, mass-market, anti-jargon. Vincent's whole brand is "personal finance, decoded" for the everyday wage-earner. His vincentchan.co hero reads "Get Smarter With Your Money in 5 Minutes a Week." That is the register - direct, low-friction, no Wall-Street vocabulary.
  • Numbers anchor the claim. His Yahoo Finance piece is "7 Strategies Vincent Chan Used To Save 73% of His Income." His newsletter is "5 Minutes a Week." The Course is "$127." Specific numbers carry the credibility, not adjectives.
  • Trajectory over theory. He references his own quit ($120K finance job, the Entrepreneur feature) and his own savings rate (the 73% Yahoo figure) - the audience trusts that he has lived the answer, not just studied it.
  • Operator-credentialed, not finfluencer-flat. Words like budget, portfolio, automation, allocation, tax efficiency, retirement vehicle belong. Words like alpha, pumping, moonshot, dump, bag-secured do not. Vincent's audience is the educated wage-earner, not the day-trading meme-account.

Brand pillars (what the copy stands on)

  1. The Wall-Street-exit credibility. Economics degree, $120K finance job, walked away to build the content brand. Press-verified (Entrepreneur). Anchors the "I have seen what your money is doing on the institutional side" credential.
  2. The ~600K-800K YouTube + 323K IG + 50K newsletter audience. Multi-platform reach proves the message converts at scale. Press features (Yahoo, Nasdaq, Entrepreneur, The Tilt) prove the brand outside the algorithm.
  3. The mass-market personal-finance positioning. "Build wealth, buy freedom" lives on his IG bio and the homepage. The cohort productises that single line into a 90-day operator-grade install.
  4. The specific-number register. 73% income saved. 5-minute weekly newsletter. $127 Course. The cohort copy mirrors this - specific seat counts, specific cycle counts, specific deliverable timing - not vague aspirational framing.
  5. The Course as proof of execution. He has shipped an evergreen $127 product. The cohort is the structural upgrade above it, not a replacement.

What the cohort offer is, in one paragraph

A 90-day operator cohort taught by Vincent Chan for high-earning professionals (W-2 $150K+ or self-employed $200K+). Two-day in-person kickoff in NYC hosted by Vincent + 90-day implementation with weekly cohort calls + office hours + private cohort room + 1:1 plan reviews at days 30, 60, 90. Curriculum builds the operator-grade personal-finance stack: budget automation, investment portfolio structure, tax efficiency, retirement vehicle selection, debt strategy. 6-8 seats per cohort. Quarterly cadence. Sits between Vincent's $127 evergreen Course and his free YouTube + newsletter content, at the price tier his audience has been asking for and currently has nowhere to buy.

Body voice rules

  • Open every section with a specific number, a specific outcome, or a specific operator deliverable. "Six seats per cycle" reads stronger than "small group."
  • Cite the channel, the newsletter, the trajectory (Economics degree, ex-Wall Street $120K, NYC base, three-year content trajectory) by name. Generic "experienced personal-finance educator" weakens.
  • Use "budget," "portfolio," "allocation," "tax efficiency," "retirement vehicle," "operator," "install," "implementation" generously - they are the cohort's vocabulary and the audience is past the basics.
  • Use the verified flagship YouTube video ("If I Started Investing In 2025, This Is What I Would Do") as the warm-up embed on the cohort landing hero, not a 1-minute manufactured promo.
  • Anchor the press credentials by publication name (Yahoo Finance, Nasdaq, Entrepreneur, The Tilt) with specific article excerpts when space allows.

Voice register guardrails

The cohort voice rejects hype-circuit, manifestation-cliche, and finfluencer-aspirational registers across the board. Where finance-niche copy tends to use aspirational framing as a hook, the cohort uses specific operator deliverables. Where peer creators use scarcity language as a close, the cohort uses real seat caps. Where finfluencer surfaces lean on return-claim language, the cohort copy stays anchored to deliverable language and to general personal-finance structure, never to extrapolated investor outcomes.

The full ban list and the cliche-vocabulary it rejects live in the internal AG1 voice handbook (Context/Marketing/copy-rules.md) and do not appear in any client-facing surface or in this strategy file.

Outcomes language

Outcome-language is anchored to specific operator deliverables, not investment returns:

  • "By Day 30 you have an automated budget system live across your accounts with category-level tracking."
  • "By Day 60 you have a portfolio allocation mapped against your risk tolerance, retirement horizon, and tax bracket - reviewed live with Vincent."
  • "By Day 90 you have a tax-efficiency plan drafted across W-2 income, tax-advantaged accounts, and taxable accounts - with the 12-month execution checklist."

Promise-language and individual-outcome extrapolation are rejected per the voice register guardrails above. Outcomes-language uses deliverable framing only.

Comparison framing (against alternative offers)

The cohort competes with:

  • DIY YouTube + free newsletter learning (the current default for Vincent's audience). The cohort beats it on speed (90 days vs years of fragmented self-study), on structure (a sequenced operator-install curriculum vs scattered videos), and on live access to Vincent across the install window.
  • The $127 Freedom Budget Accelerator (Vincent's own entry-tier product). The cohort beats this on depth (operator-grade stack vs entry-tier budget framework), on live access, on the in-person NYC kickoff, and on the 1:1 plan reviews. The Course remains the right product for someone not at the cohort income threshold; the cohort is the upgrade for someone past it.
  • Comparable peer high-tickets (Ramit Sethi Money Coaching ~$2K, Andrei Jikh premium tier, Graham Stephan Real Estate / YouTube academies). The cohort beats these on Vincent-specific access (Ramit is bigger but the senior tier is harder to get into Ramit-live access), on the operator-stack curriculum (Andrei Jikh leans investing-heavy, Graham leans real-estate or YouTube-creator, Vincent's lane is the full personal-finance operator stack), and on the in-person NYC kickoff format.
  • 1:1 financial advisor (~$200-$400/hour or AUM-fee structures). The cohort beats this on price-per-hour-of-value (90 days of cohort access + 1:1 reviews costs less than 30-50 hours of advisor time), and on the educational depth (advisors execute, the cohort teaches the operator to execute themselves).

Compliance line on landing-page copy

Anywhere the cohort touches the topic of money outcomes:

  • Outcomes vary by capital, market, and operator execution
  • The cohort teaches the operator stack; it does not guarantee specific returns or income
  • Vincent Chan is not a registered investment adviser; the cohort is personal-finance education, not regulated investment advisory
  • The mandatory footer line ships on the landing page, the VSL, and the cohort terms: "Not investment advice. Results vary by capital, market, and operator execution."

This language ships in the landing-page footer and the cohort terms. It is also the line every ad creative writes to.
