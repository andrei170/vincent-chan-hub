Built: 2026-05-16 Frame: Five-email warm nurture sequence. Loaded into the ESP. Triggered on cohort Typeform submission OR Course alumni 30 days post-purchase. Routes the lead toward The Wealth Operator Cohort application.

Sequence overview

Five emails over 12 days. Warm posture throughout. The reader has either just applied to the cohort (variant A) or just completed the $127 Course 30+ days ago (variant B). The sequence is the bridge from "I am interested" to "approved and enrolled."

Email 1 - Day 0

Subject: What the cohort is, and the one question to answer first

Pre-header: Plus the operator-stack readiness gate I run on every application

Hi {First name},

Thanks for taking the time to look at The Wealth Operator Cohort.

Quick context on what this is. The cohort is a 90-day operator install of the personal-finance stack I have been running and teaching publicly across YouTube, the Sunday newsletter, and the Freedom Budget Accelerator. Two days in person in New York for the kickoff. Ninety days of weekly cohort calls, office hours, a private cohort room, and 1:1 plan reviews at days 30, 60, and 90. Six to eight operators per cycle. Quarterly cadence.

The curriculum is the operator-grade stack: budget automation across accounts, portfolio allocation by your specific income and timeline, tax efficiency (W-2 or self-employed, tax-advantaged or taxable), retirement vehicle selection (401K / Roth / HSA / brokerage), and debt strategy. By Day 90 you have an automated budget system live, a portfolio allocation mapped against your specific risk and timeline, and a tax-efficiency plan drafted with the 12-month execution checklist.

The one question to answer first is the income gate. The cohort is built for W-2 earners at $150K+ or self-employed operators at $200K+. The reason is structural - the operator-stack mechanics (Roth conversion ladders, tax-loss harvesting cadence, HSA-as-retirement allocation) start earning their weight at that income level. Below it, the $127 Freedom Budget Accelerator is the right product. Above it, the cohort is.

If you are at or above the income gate, the application form sorts the rest. We review applications inside 48 hours.

More on what the 90 days actually look like in the next email.

  • Vincent

Voice notes:

  • No hype language, no aspirational framing, no manifestation register
  • Specific references (six-to-eight seats, days 30 / 60 / 90, $150K / $200K income gate) anchored once each
  • The income-gate framing pre-qualifies before the application weight lands
  • The Course mentioned by name as the right-fit alternative below the cohort tier - kills the "is the Course enough" objection early

Email 2 - Day 2

Subject: Why I built this above the Course, not as a replacement

Pre-header: The structural reason the operator stack does not fit in an evergreen $127 product

{First name},

A few people have asked why the cohort sits above the Freedom Budget Accelerator instead of replacing it.

Honest answer - the Course works for the audience tier it was built for. Someone earning $50K-$150K, building the entry-tier personal-finance stack, working through the six modules at their own pace, applying the framework with the tools and the FAR Method built in. The $127 price reflects the work. The lifetime access reflects the format. It does what it was built to do.

The operator stack at $150K+ is a different problem. The mechanics get more specific, the trade-offs get more individual, and the questions stop having one-size-fits-all answers. A Roth conversion ladder for a single-income W-2 household earning $180K runs differently than the same ladder for a self-employed operator at $250K with an HSA-eligible plan. A taxable brokerage rebalancing cadence at $400K assets runs differently than at $80K. The questions get personal at this tier, the answers need live access to someone running the same stack, and the format that works is cohort-shaped - not evergreen-video-shaped.

The cohort builds the stack with you, on your specific numbers, with live calls every week for 90 days. The 2-day NYC kickoff is where most of the operator-stack mapping happens - the rest of the 90 days is the implementation work with weekly cohort accountability.

If your stack is past where the Course can take it, the cohort is the next surface. If it is not yet, the Course is the right move and the cohort opens later.

  • Vincent

Voice notes:

  • Specific income-band examples (single-income W-2 $180K, self-employed $250K with HSA, $400K assets) ground the framing
  • The Course is positioned with respect, not as a downgrade - protects existing alumni trust
  • Cohort vs Course is framed as fit, not hierarchy

Email 3 - Day 5

Subject: What 90 days of operator-stack install actually looks like

Pre-header: The 2-day NYC kickoff + the weekly cadence + the three 1:1 reviews

{First name},

Here is what the 90 days actually look like, week by week.

The 2-day in-person kickoff in NYC. Six to eight operators in the room. Day one maps the operator stack across all members - current state, gaps, near-term moves. Day two locks the Day-30 plan for each operator and the 90-day execution map. Accountability pairings assigned. The cohort room (private Circle or Slack) goes live before you leave.

Weeks 2-4 - automated budget system live. Weekly cohort call covers the five-account budget framework, sinking-fund construction, automated transfers, and the operator's review cadence. By Day 30, the system is live across your accounts. The Day-30 1:1 review confirms it and locks the Day-60 plan.

Weeks 5-8 - portfolio allocation mapped. Weekly cohort calls cover risk-band assessment, tax-advantaged vs taxable allocation, the retirement vehicle stack (401K / Roth / HSA / brokerage), and rebalancing cadence. By Day 60, your portfolio allocation is mapped against your specific income, timeline, and risk tolerance. The Day-60 1:1 review confirms it and locks the Day-90 plan.

Weeks 9-12 - tax-efficiency plan drafted. Weekly cohort calls cover tax-loss harvesting cadence, the Roth conversion ladder timing window, debt strategy (mortgage / HELOC / refi), and the 12-month operator review cadence. By Day 90, your tax-efficiency plan is drafted and the 12-month execution checklist is locked. The Day-90 1:1 review hands you the alumni-room access and the quarterly refresh-call cadence.

Office hours run weekly the entire 90 days. The private cohort room is always-on. Alumni access continues post-cohort.

That is the 90 days. The application form is on the cohort page.

  • Vincent

Voice notes:

  • This is the curriculum-detail email; the specifics make the $10K-$18K price defensible
  • Day 30 / 60 / 90 anchored deliverables (automated budget live / portfolio allocation mapped / tax-efficiency plan drafted) are concrete, not aspirational
  • The NYC kickoff is named explicitly - travel logistics question handled in Email 4

Email 4 - Day 8

Subject: The kind of operator the cohort is built for

Pre-header: The income gate, the readiness gate, and the operator-stack maturity bands

{First name},

A few self-qualifying notes on who fits the cohort and who does not.

Income gate. W-2 earners at $150K+ or self-employed operators at $200K+. The reason is structural - the mechanics start earning their weight at that income level, and the cohort runs deep on tax efficiency and retirement-vehicle selection that needs the income for the math to work.

Stack maturity. Three bands fit cleanly:

  • Band one - entry operator. You have automated transfers in some form but the system is messy. You have a 401K and maybe a Roth IRA. No structured taxable brokerage. No tax-loss harvesting cadence. No structured emergency fund. The cohort builds the operator stack from your current state to mapped-and-running across 90 days.
  • Band two - mid-career operator. You have most of the operator stack in place but the pieces are not integrated. The budget runs separately from the portfolio. The retirement vehicles are open but the contribution prioritisation is unclear. Tax efficiency is reactive, not planned. The cohort integrates the stack and adds the tax-efficiency and rebalancing layers.
  • Band three - high-income operator. You have the full stack in place and the question is optimisation - the Roth conversion ladder timing, the HSA stacking, the tax-loss harvesting cadence at scale, the multi-account rebalancing. The cohort runs the optimisation layer and the 1:1 plan reviews lean into your specific edge cases.

Logistics. The 2-day NYC kickoff is the live anchor. Flight + 2 nights hotel is on you. We have a couple of co-discounted hotel partners we send to applicants on approval. The kickoff dates rotate quarterly - the next cycle dates ship on the application response email.

If any of the three bands fit, the application form is the right next move.

  • Vincent

Voice notes:

  • The three-band self-qualification kills the "am I a fit" objection while pre-segmenting applications for review
  • Logistics (NYC flight + hotel + co-discounted partners) handled flat - no surprises post-deposit
  • No countdown timer, no manufactured urgency

Email 5 - Day 12

Subject: Cohort cycle dates and how to lock the seat

Pre-header: Six to eight seats per cycle. Quarterly cadence. Application is on the page.

{First name},

Closing the sequence with the practical stuff.

The cohort runs four times a year - quarterly cadence. Each cycle caps at six to eight seats to keep the in-person kickoff intimate and the weekly cohort calls real. The next cycle dates ship on the application-response email after approval.

Cycle mechanics:

  • Cycles fill in order of paid deposits

Pricing sits between the free YouTube + newsletter content at the bottom and a bespoke 1:1 financial advisor relationship at the top. Specific pricing ships on the application-response email - the band is published on the cohort page.

The application form: {Cohort landing URL}

That is the offer. The work begins on Day 1 of the NYC kickoff.

  • Vincent

P.S. If a specific cycle date does not work for you, the application form lets you flag it - we hold seats across cycles for confirmed-but-flexible applicants on a rolling basis.

Voice notes:

  • This is the close email; still no hype
  • Specific pricing not stated (price ladder hinted, exact band lives on the landing page only)
  • No "limited spots remaining" countdown - the seat cap is real, not manufactured scarcity
  • The P.S. captures cycle-flexible interest from leads who are sized for the cohort but not the immediate cycle
